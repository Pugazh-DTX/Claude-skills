# Editorial rules

Apply these rules to every piece of public copy, including the post, hooks, carousel text, CTA, and hashtags.

## Proper nouns, brand names, and abbreviations

Write company names, product names, file extensions, and technical terms exactly as their owners write them. Never lowercase, space out, or remove punctuation from a proper name.

Examples of correct usage:

- D-ID not "d id" or "D ID"
- .glb not "glb" or "GLB"
- GPT-4 not "gpt4" or "GPT 4"
- Three.js not "threejs" or "Three JS"
- CodeDTX not "codedtx" or "Code DTX"

On the first use of any acronym, initialisation, or technical abbreviation, write the full form followed by the short form in parentheses. On all later uses, the short form alone is acceptable.

Example: Graphics Language Transmission Format (.glb) files store 3D models and animations. Most developers export a .glb and expect it to look good immediately.

Do not invent expansions. If the full form is not known with certainty, use only the short form as given and do not guess.

## No dashes

Do not use hyphens, en dashes, or em dashes. Rewrite the sentence instead.

This rule does not apply to code, file names, repository paths, private notes, or source citations.

Run a character check before delivery for:

```text
-
–
—
```

## Human voice

Write like an experienced person explaining something useful to a peer.

Use natural spoken language, contractions where appropriate, varied sentence length, and concrete words. Avoid stiff transitions, symmetrical phrasing, generic inspiration, fake personal stories, and writing that sounds generated.

Read the copy aloud. If a line sounds unnatural, rewrite it.

## Easy to understand

Use one idea per sentence. Prefer familiar words. Explain or remove technical terms. Use short paragraphs. Make the business meaning clear when the source is technical.

Do not assume the reader knows the project, codebase, tools, or acronyms.

## Hook

Make the first one or two lines earn attention through one of these patterns:

1. A surprising but verified observation
2. A useful tension or contrast
3. A clear question the reader recognizes
4. A practical consequence
5. A proven number or exact framework count

Never use fake urgency, fear, vague hype, or engagement bait.

Avoid openings such as:

```text
In today's fast paced world
AI is changing everything
We are thrilled to announce
Game changing
Revolutionary
```

## Post format

Group two to three related sentences into a paragraph. Do not add a blank line after every sentence.

Use a blank line only when shifting to a clearly different thought, such as moving from the problem to the insight, or from the insight to the lesson. A standard post needs three to five blank line breaks total across the whole piece.

Single sentence paragraphs are acceptable for the hook and the closing question. Avoid stacking more than two single sentence paragraphs anywhere else.

Never produce a post where every line stands alone with a blank line above and below it. Read the formatted output and collapse any section where two or more consecutive single sentence paragraphs could flow naturally as one paragraph.

## Tone

Educate first. Keep commercial relevance subtle. Show the decision, trade off, failure, constraint, or lesson behind the work.

Use no more than three to five relevant hashtags. Do not end every post with the same question.
